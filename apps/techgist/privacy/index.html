Privacy Policy

Techgist · Last updated November 16, 2026

Techgist has no accounts, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking. What you read and save stays on your iPhone. If you turn on big story alerts in version 2.0 or later, our server keeps your phone's notification address so it can send them. That is the only information about you or your device we hold.

Privacy question? Email us

Overview

Techgist is an iPhone news reader for technology, AI, startup and developer news. This policy describes version 2.0 and later. What was different in earlier versions is at the end.

Information stored on your device

The app stores the stories you save, which stories you have read, the sources you muted, your notification and reading settings, and a cache of recent stories and pictures so the app opens quickly and works offline. All of it lives in Techgist's own storage on your device. We do not receive it and cannot see it.

Information sent to Techgist's server (only with big story alerts on)

When you turn on "Big story alerts" and allow notifications, the app sends our server:

  • the push notification token Apple issues for Techgist on your device (a device identifier used only to deliver notifications to this app)
  • your time zone, so alerts are not sent at night
  • your language and region setting (for example en_US)
  • the app's version number

We use this only to send you big story alerts. It is not linked to your name, email, an account or anything else that identifies you, it is not used for advertising or tracking, and it is not sold or shared. The server also records which alerts it has already sent to that token so it does not send one twice.

When you turn "Big story alerts" off, or take away the notification permission, the app tells the server to delete the token and everything stored with it. Tokens Apple reports as no longer valid are deleted as well.

The server is a virtual server rented from Hostinger, located in India, and run only by Techgist's developer. Alerts travel through Apple's Push Notification service.

If you turned big story alerts on in an earlier version, version 2.0 asks you once before it sends anything to the server, and sends nothing if you turn alerts off.

If you never turn on big story alerts, nothing is sent to our server.

Where stories come from

The app downloads the current list of stories, and the pictures on the cards, from GitHub (raw.githubusercontent.com), where Techgist publishes them. If that list is unavailable or out of date, the app downloads public news feeds directly from the publishers, such as techcrunch.com and theverge.com, and pictures then load from the publishers' servers.

These are ordinary web requests. Like any website, GitHub, the publishers and our server receive standard request information such as your IP address, under their own privacy policies. The requests contain nothing about what you read or saved.

Summaries

The short summaries on the cards are written by an AI model on Techgist's server from the publishers' articles, before the stories reach your phone. Nothing from your device is sent to any AI service.

Notifications

The morning gist is scheduled on your device and involves no server. Big story alerts are sent by Techgist's server as described above. Both are off until you turn them on, and each can be turned off in the app's Settings.

Information we do not collect

  • No name, email address, account, or contact information
  • No reading history, saved stories, or preferences
  • No location
  • No advertising identifier
  • No analytics or diagnostics
  • No payment or financial information

Reading full articles

When you open a story, it loads in Safari's in-app browser directly from the publisher. That publisher's own privacy policy applies to its website.

Third parties

Techgist contains no third-party software development kits and shares no data about you with third parties.

Retention and deletion

Saved stories and settings stay on your device until you delete the app. The notification token on our server is deleted when you turn big story alerts off. To have it removed another way, email us and turn alerts off; we cannot look up a token by name because we hold no names.

Children's privacy

Techgist does not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Versions before 2.0

In versions 1.0 to 1.2, big story alerts were created on your device and nothing was sent to Techgist's server, so no notification token was held. On iPhones with Apple Intelligence those versions could also write summaries on the device. Everything else in this policy applies to them as well.

Changes to this policy

If Techgist's data practices change, this page will be updated before the app version that changes them is released. The date at the top shows the last revision.
